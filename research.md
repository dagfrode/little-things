# Research notes

These notes back up the recommendations in `data/items.json`. Each item has a `sources` list whose ids
match the ones below. Notes were collected October 2026.

## The short version

1. **The best "toy" is a person.** For babies especially, the main value of play with objects is the
   back-and-forth with a caregiver, not the object's "educational" content. [aap-toys], [aap-play]
2. **Simple beats electronic.** Parents talk less, and less responsively, when a toy does the talking.
   Blocks, puzzles and books lead to more conversation than electronic toys. [sosa], [aap-toys]
3. **Fewer is better.** Toddlers with 4 toys available played longer and more inventively with each one than
   toddlers with 16. So this is a list to pick *one or two* things from, not a shopping list. [dauch]
4. **Everyday objects are real play material.** Babies explore stainless bowls, wooden spoons, boxes and
   fabric with all their senses (heuristic play, "treasure baskets"). Open-ended, varied materials invite more
   invention. [goldschmied], [nicholson]
5. **Kids love the real thing.** Given the choice, 3–6-year-olds preferred doing real activities (cutting real
   vegetables, real phone calls) to pretend versions, and played about twice as long with real objects as with toy
   copies. Real child-size tools and a step stool to reach the counter are therefore good gifts. [taggart], [montessori]
6. **Pretend play still has a place.** The evidence that pretend play boosts intelligence or creativity is weak,
   but it is linked to language, storytelling, social skills and self-regulation. [lillard]
7. **Blocks and puzzles build spatial skills,** which predict later maths. [verdine], [levine]
8. **Reading from birth** builds vocabulary and later reading skills. Board books are always a safe gift. [literacy]
9. **Movement matters from day one.** Tummy time (at least 30 min a day, spread out) is linked to earlier motor
   milestones. Babies under 1 year should be physically active several times a day, and children aged 1–4 should
   have at least 180 minutes of activity a day. [hewitt], [who-activity]

## Developmental phases used on the site

Phases follow the CDC/AAP 2022 milestone checklists (milestones that ≥75 % of children reach by that age) [cdc].
The play notes are ours, derived from the sources above.

| Phase | Ages | What's happening | What play looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Newborn | 0–3 m | Focuses on faces, high contrast; lifts head on tummy | Faces, voices, black-and-white pictures, tummy time, a mirror |
| Reaching | 3–6 m | Reaches and grasps, brings things to mouth, rolls | Rattles, textures, things to grab and mouth, play mats |
| Sitting | 6–9 m | Sits, transfers between hands, bangs objects; peekaboo | Treasure basket, banging bowls, scarves, peekaboo |
| Crawling | 9–12 m | Crawls, pulls up; pincer grasp; looks for hidden things (object permanence) [piaget] | Put-in/take-out, object permanence box, boxes to crawl into |
| First steps | 12–18 m | Walks, stacks 2+ blocks, scribbles, first words, "helps" | Push/pull toys, stacking, crayons, books, real cups and spoons |
| Little helper | 18–24 m | Runs, kicks, simple pretend (feeds a doll), 2-word phrases | Pretend kitchen, real chores, puzzles, ball play |
| Pretend & build | 2–3 y | Climbs, uses things in pretend, follows 2-step instructions | Blocks, play dough, water and sand, dress-up |
| Big imagination | 3–5 y | Tells stories, draws people, takes turns in games, counts | Board games, art supplies, real tools, construction, balance bike |

A child's age is only a rough guide. Children move through these phases at their own pace, and most items work
across several phases.

## Safety rules applied to every item

- **Choking (under 3 years, and older children who still mouth things):** anything that fits in a toilet-roll tube
  (the ~3.2 cm small-parts cylinder used in toy regulation) is a choking hazard. Balls should be bigger than ~4.5 cm. [cpsc-small]
- **Never:** button batteries, loose magnets, balloons, long cords or strings (>30 cm) near babies. [cpsc-small]
- **Plastics:** EU rules ban the most harmful phthalates (DEHP, DBP, BBP) in all toys, and DINP, DIDP and DNOP in
  toys that can go in the mouth. Even so, an EU enforcement project found restricted phthalates in about 1 in 5 toys
  tested, mostly cheap soft-PVC toys bought outside major retailers. Buying from established EU/Norwegian stores lowers
  that risk. Stainless steel, wood, untreated cotton and natural rubber avoid the issue altogether. [eu-phthalates]
- Everyday objects aren't safety-tested as toys. Check for sharp edges, loose parts and whether the item can break,
  and stay close by.

## How the "plastic" filter is defined

- `none`: no plastic in the item (metal, wood, glass, paper, cotton, wool, natural rubber, wax).
- `some`: mostly non-plastic but with plastic parts, or a soft toy with polyester stuffing.
- `plastic`: mainly plastic, silicone or synthetic fabric.

## Where the items link to

- **IKEA Norway**: direct product links, with prices fetched from IKEA's search API (see `scripts/refresh-ikea.py`).
- **Other Norwegian stores** (Clas Ohlson, Europris, Nille, Jollyroom, Barnas Hus, Lekmer, Norli, Panduro): links to
  a store search, so they keep working when a specific product sells out or is replaced.
- **FINN.no**: secondhand search, a good fit for wooden toys and the plastic you'd rather not buy new.
- **Amazon.se**: only for items that are hard to find in Norway.
